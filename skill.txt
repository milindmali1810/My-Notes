SKILL: Founder Voice Extraction & Transfer
================================================================
Purpose: Given a corpus of a founder's writing, extract their voice
as a reusable set of rules, then apply those rules to write NEW
content (different topic, different data) that reads as if that
same person wrote it.

--------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 1 — READ THE FULL CORPUS BEFORE EXTRACTING ANYTHING
--------------------------------------------------------------
Do not extract voice from a summary, a single post, or memory of
"how this type of person usually writes." Read every piece in the
sample. Voice patterns that appear once are noise; patterns that
repeat 3+ times across different pieces are signal.

--------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 2 — EXTRACT STRUCTURE (macro level)
--------------------------------------------------------------
For each piece, note:
- What is the opening move? (scene, claim, question, confession)
- Where does the thesis land — first line or several paragraphs in?
- How does it close? (resolution, open question, deflection,
  call to action)
- What's the paragraph rhythm — short and stacked, or long and
  discursive?

--------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 3 — EXTRACT LEXICAL TICS (micro level)
--------------------------------------------------------------
Pull VERBATIM recurring phrases and sentence templates, not
paraphrases of them. Examples of the kind of thing to look for:
- Hedging phrases used before a strong claim
  ("I want to be precise about...")
- Self-correction or reframing structures
  ("I'm not saying X. I'm saying Y.")
- How they handle numbers — bare, or always paired with a
  comparison/benchmark?
- How they close — question aimed at the reader, or question the
  writer admits is still open for themselves?
- Sentence length pattern at moments of emphasis (short flat
  fragments vs. longer qualified sentences)
- Any recurring self-deprecating or narrative-deflating move
  ("this is not a particularly inspiring story")

--------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 4 — SEPARATE VOICE FROM CONTENT
--------------------------------------------------------------
Voice = the mechanics above. Content = the specific facts, numbers,
and story of the ORIGINAL corpus. When transferring voice to new
material, only Steps 2 and 3 travel. Never let details from the
original person's story (their numbers, their history, their
product facts) leak into the new piece.

--------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 5 — APPLY TO NEW MATERIAL
--------------------------------------------------------------
Write the new piece using:
- The new person's REAL data and facts (never invent specifics —
  a fabricated number or unverified detail breaks the voice-transfer
  exercise even if it "sounds right," because precision without
  actually verifying it is the opposite of what most rigorous
  founder voices are built on)
- The extracted structural pattern from Step 2
- The extracted lexical patterns from Step 3, used at the same
  rate/density they appeared in the original corpus — not more,
  not less

--------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 6 — VERIFY BEFORE DELIVERING
--------------------------------------------------------------
Check the output against the checklist:
[ ] Does the opening move match the extracted pattern (not just
    "some conflict"), specifically?
[ ] Are lexical tics present verbatim/near-verbatim, at a natural
    density, not forced into every paragraph?
[ ] Does every number in the piece belong to the person it's about,
    verified — not invented to sound like the source voice?
[ ] Does the ending match the extracted closing pattern precisely
    (e.g., an unresolved question owned by the writer, not a
    rhetorical hook)?
[ ] Would someone who has read the source corpus recognize the
    voice without being told?

--------------------------------------------------------------
COMMON FAILURE MODES
--------------------------------------------------------------
- Mimicking tone/mood ("confident," "vulnerable") instead of
  mimicking specific, repeatable sentence structures.
- Borrowing structure only (scene-open, question-close) while
  skipping the lexical tics — produces something "in the spirit of"
  the voice but not actually in it.
- Inventing specificity (exact percentages, channel breakdowns) to
  match the source's habit of precision, without confirming the
  new person's real numbers. This is the single most common failure
  — it looks like fidelity to the voice but is actually a fabrication
  the source person's own rigor would never have allowed.
